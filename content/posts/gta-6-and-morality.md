GTA 6, the game, launched a few days back and as expected, it has exploded in popularity. Ever since the launch of the game, the internet has been divided into two separate factions. One says that the game promotes violence, drugs and other such serious criminal activities. The other says that it is just a game, nobody starts committing violence in the real world because of a game. Let's have a nuanced discussion on this.<br><br>

For someone who does not know what GTA 6 (or in general, the whole GTA series of games) is, it is an open-world game where you play as a criminal who gets missions that usually involve some serious criminal activity. It includes all kinds of serious crimes like bombing places, killing people, drug smuggling, and the list goes on. The game is known for its hyper-realistic graphics and storyline. It lets you live a gangster's life. It has other things apart from missions. I have observed that some people just roam around in cars that they have stolen from an NPC (non-player character, basically a fictional character inside the game) and they keep on smashing anything that comes in their way. It could be something like streetlights or even people walking on the footpath.<br><br>

I found it very interesting that such a violent gaming franchise has become one of the most popular in the entire history of gaming. Why do people enjoy such games? What is the psychology of the people who play such violent games, or more generally, consume and enjoy violent content (this includes games as well as movies)? Let's brainstorm that together.<br><br>

I think the people who buy games like these can be divided into multiple categories: 
1. The people who buy it completely due to FOMO.
2. The people who buy it due to nostalgia for the previous iterations of the game.
3. The people who buy it because they actually enjoy the game in itself.

I don't think any player will fit completely into one bucket, but such categorization helps in our analysis, so we can work with this for now. The category I am most interested in is the third one, the people who enjoy the game in itself. So let's dissect this category further. Going forward we will talk only about category 3 of players.<br><br>

There is a lot of debate online on whether such games should exist or not from a moral standpoint. So let's look at what a player actually enjoys when they say that they are enjoying the game. One camp on the internet says that people get sadistic pleasure from such games. These people say that players of such games have a mentality similar to a real-life criminal. The only distinction is that these people fear the consequences of committing such crimes in real life and so this is an escape for them where they can fulfill their sadistic pleasure without bearing the consequences for it. In case these people get absolute or near absolute freedom, they will commit heinous crimes in real life as well. Just to provide a concrete example to the argument of this camp of the internet, if this kind of GTA 6 player becomes a billionaire with almost unlimited power, they might go to a place like Epstein Island. This viewpoint, in my opinion, is too extreme.<br><br>

A milder version of this viewpoint exists. It has to do with abstraction (check out my blog on [Abstractions](/posts/abstractions)). Real-life violence requires going against your instinct. Listening to the victim's screams, for example, and acting despite that without empathy, in a cold-blooded fashion. A game like this removes all the friction. It makes treating the victim as an object rather than a subject extremely easy. This is like a dictator ordering his soldiers to massacre hundreds of people. The actual screams of the victims are abstracted from the perpetrator of the crime, i.e., the dictator. Here the player of the game treats people like objects, but the aesthetics of committing an actual crime do not suit them. This is similar to how many non-vegetarians prefer not to kill the chicken themselves for aesthetic reasons.<br><br>

An even milder view suggests that players aren't seeking sadistic pleasure at all; rather, they are indifferent to the crimes and they are just enjoying the thrill of the game, like a fast car chase or something similar to that. It has nothing to do with sadism.<br><br>

Some people suggest that the realistic graphics and storyline are genuinely enjoyable, similar to how violent movies can have a really enjoyable storyline, dialogues and background music. It has nothing to do with promoting violence in society or even the corruption of the mind of the individual who is playing the game.<br><br>

Some also suggest that the absolute freedom the game offers provides an escape from the restrictions that come with staying in the real world. It is a way to vent out the aggression (whether this is positive or negative, I will come to that at the end of this article).<br><br>

In my opinion, there is some merit to all of these arguments. All of them have some strong points and some weak points. For me, there are two dimensions we can analyze such games or media in general. First is impact on the society, and second is psychology of the individual playing that game or consuming that media. For the society, there is no conclusive relation observed between violent behavior and violent video games in [research](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2020/03/violent-video-games-behavior) conducted (although it does find a small link to everyday aggression like yelling and pushing). In my opinion, businesses produce what the people demand. Such games and media exist because people demand it. But also, it is not that simple. Media is used to spread propaganda and narratives. It is well known that entertainment media play an important role in shaping the society. So it is a feedback loop between the society and the entertainment media.<br><br>

The individual psychology, especially the moral aspect of it is much more complicated. I personally don't think most people play these games for sadistic pleasure; there might be some minority of players like that, but I think (and I really hope) that is not true. I think this is similar to how a non-drinker can vibe on songs like "Chaar Botal Vodka". It is also not necessary that these people are frustrated by the restriction imposed on them by the society of not drinking and secretly want to drink and hence they enjoy songs like these. Although some people may be like that, many also enjoy the beats and the tune of the song and ignore the lyrics. Enjoyment in GTA might be similar; people do not necessarily enjoy the sadistic pleasure, they just enjoy the thrill.<br><br>

The escape from real-world restrictions and venting out aggression argument is very interesting to me because it can be either moral or immoral depending on what your beliefs are. There are two schools of thought in this. One says that since this aggression does not harm anyone, it is not immoral. It leads to a harmless venting out of frustration. The second one says that the player fails to maintain restraint and needs an outlet for their aggression; they are unable to contain their aggression. They are still impulsive, just the receiver of the aggression is not a sentient being. And that giving in to impulses part makes it immoral for this school of thought. The first one is called the _harm-based_ school of thought and the second one is called _virtue ethics_.<br><br>

To explain these better, imagine two people - Person A and Person B. Both are extremely frustrated and are in a very similar mindset. Person A shouts at their building watchman for no reason just to vent out their frustration. Person B goes into the gym and keeps on hitting the punching bag like crazy. They are also doing it to vent out their frustration. Person A is acting immorally for both the schools of thought. But Person B is acting immorally only for the virtue ethics school of thought. The virtue ethics school defines morality not only by who or what is at the receiving end, but also by what the actor is thinking. Some Indian schools of thought that are closer to virtue ethics categorize even a violent thought as violence. According to them, Person A commits two moral failures (losing self-control as well as injustice to another sentient being), whereas Person B only falls short of ideal inner self-mastery. So whether playing GTA to vent aggression is moral or not depends on which school of thought you belong to. Because of the influence of Jainism's _ahimsa_ on me, I personally find myself leaning towards virtue ethics in many situations in life. But I also try to keep in mind that the other definition of morality could be equally valid and not to judge anyone based on their preferences of entertainment media.<br><br>
